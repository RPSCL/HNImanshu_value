Owner earnings, the Buffett way

Buffett values a business by the cash its owners could take out each year without hurting it (his 1986 letter calls this “owner earnings”), discounted back to today.

Year by year, from the annual reports

  • Capex = change in net block + change in capital work in progress + depreciation (never below 0)
  • Maintenance capex = the smaller of capex and depreciation: what it costs just to stand still
  • Owner earnings = cash from operations − maintenance capex (working capital is already inside operating cash)
  • Strict free cash flow = cash from operations − all capex, growth spending included

Value per share

  • Base: average of the last 3 years, so one lumpy year doesn't decide it
  • Growth: the lower of 5-year revenue and profit growth, kept between 0% and 15%
  • Years 1–5 at that growth, years 6–10 at half of it, then 4% a year for ever, all discounted at 12%
  • Borrowings minus investments come off (net cash is added), then ÷ shares today
  • Buy below keeps a 25% margin of safety under the value

Left out or treated differently

  • Banks, NBFCs and insurers: their cash flow isn't a fair measure, so no value
  • Oil & gas, metals & mining and realty (marked CYC): a 5-year average and no growth, since a boom year shouldn't be paid for twice

Backtest

On 1 July of each year since 2019 (all March results are out by then) it values every stock with only the reports known on that day, buys the ones furthest below value in equal amounts and holds them for a year, against the NIFTY 500. It uses today's index lists and prices only (no dividends, costs or taxes), so treat it as a rough guide. Past results don't promise future ones.
